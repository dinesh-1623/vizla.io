# Blocked Vehicles - Detailed Documentation

## Blocked Vehicles Management System - Complete Guide

This document provides comprehensive explanations for all features, statuses, and workflows in the Blocked Vehicles system, including their purpose, importance, and operational impact.

---

## **Overview**

### Purpose:
The Blocked Vehicles system manages vehicles that have been located by spotters but cannot be immediately recovered due to physical access restrictions, legal holds, or other obstacles. It tracks blocked vehicles, documents the blocking reasons, monitors resolution progress, and coordinates actions needed to eventually recover each vehicle.

### Why This Matters:
Blocked vehicles represent identified opportunities that cannot yet be converted to revenue:

- **Lost revenue** - Vehicle located but not recovered = no payment
- **Time sensitivity** - Vehicle may move before block is resolved
- **Resource planning** - Need to know when vehicles become available
- **Client communication** - Explain why recovery is delayed
- **Strategic decisions** - Determine if blocked vehicle is worth pursuing
- **Capacity tracking** - Blocked vehicles tie up spotter resources

### Impact on Operations:
Without a blocked vehicles system, spotted vehicles fall into a black hole:
- No tracking of why recovery failed
- No follow-up to resolve blocking issues
- Missed opportunities when blocks are resolved
- Frustrated clients wondering about delays
- Wasted spotter effort with no recovery

With a robust blocked vehicles system:
- Every block is documented and tracked
- Resolution actions are coordinated
- Vehicles are recovered when blocks clear
- Clients understand delays and see progress
- Data reveals patterns (e.g., "80% of blocks are fences - invest in coordination tools")

## **Blocking Reasons**

### **1. Behind Vehicle**

#### Purpose:
Target vehicle is physically blocked by one or more other vehicles preventing tow truck access.

#### Description:
The vehicle you need to recover is parked in a position where another vehicle (or vehicles) is blocking access. Most commonly occurs in driveways, parking lots, or street parking where the target vehicle is boxed in.

#### Common Scenarios:
- **Driveway**: Target in back, other car blocking front
- **Tandem parking**: Target in rear spot, another vehicle in front spot
- **Street parking**: Target parallel parked between two vehicles
- **Parking lot**: Target surrounded by other vehicles
- **Garage**: Target deep in garage, other vehicles blocking exit

#### Why This Matters:
Behind-vehicle blocks are:
- **Most common** - 40-50% of all blocks
- **Often resolvable** - Just need other vehicle moved
- **Time-dependent** - May clear naturally (owner leaves for work)
- **Negotiation opportunity** - Can sometimes request vehicle movement
- **Scheduling challenge** - Must coordinate return visit timing

#### Resolution Strategies:
1. **Contact blocking vehicle owner** - Request voluntary movement
2. **Wait for natural clearing** - Owner drives away for work/errands
3. **Schedule return visit** - Come back when blocking vehicle likely gone
4. **Use dollies** - In some cases, can dolly target vehicle around blocker
5. **Client coordination** - If debtor owns both vehicles, may cooperate

#### Operational Impact:
Behind-vehicle blocks have:
- **High resolution rate** - 70-80% eventually recoverable
- **Variable timing** - May resolve in hours or days
- **Low additional cost** - Just need return visit
- **Moderate frustration** - Requires patience and coordination

#### Best Practices:
- Document blocking vehicle description and plate
- Note blocking vehicle patterns (always there at night, gone during day)
- Attempt contact with blocker if safe and appropriate
- Schedule return visits for optimal times (early morning if blocker works days)
- Update status when blocking vehicle patterns change

### **2. Behind Fence**

#### Purpose:
Target vehicle is located behind a locked fence, gate, or other security barrier preventing tow truck entry.

#### Description:
The vehicle is visible and located but is secured behind a physical barrier that prevents access. Common in gated communities, fenced properties, locked compounds, and secured parking areas.

#### Common Scenarios:
- **Gated communities** - Electronic or manned gate entry
- **Fenced yards** - Chain-link or wooden fence with locked gate
- **Commercial compounds** - Industrial/warehouse security fencing
- **Apartment complexes** - Gated parking areas
- **Storage facilities** - Fenced and locked vehicle storage
- **Construction sites** - Temporary security fencing

#### Why This Matters:
Behind-fence blocks are:
- **Access-dependent** - Require code, key, or permission
- **Legally sensitive** - Cannot just cut fence or force entry
- **Relationship-driven** - Need cooperation from property owner/manager
- **Client-specific** - Some clients have legal access rights, others don't
- **Investment required** - May need legal process to gain access

#### Resolution Strategies:
1. **Obtain gate code** - From resident, property manager, or client records
2. **Property owner coordination** - Request access permission
3. **Legal access** - Client may have court order or lien holder rights
4. **Resident assistance** - Follow resident through gate (if legal)
5. **Law enforcement coordination** - Police escort for legal repossessions
6. **Timing strategy** - Access when gate is open (business hours, delivery times)

#### Operational Impact:
Behind-fence blocks have:
- **Moderate resolution rate** - 50-60% eventually recoverable
- **Longer timelines** - Days to weeks to obtain access
- **Variable cost** - May need legal fees or property access agreements
- **Higher complexity** - Requires coordination with multiple parties

#### Legal Considerations:
- **Breach of peace** - Cannot forcibly enter locked areas
- **Trespassing** - Need permission or legal right to enter
- **Property damage** - Cutting fence/breaking lock is illegal
- **Client liability** - Some clients have legal access rights, verify first

#### Best Practices:
- Document fence type, lock type, and access points
- Photograph fence and gate for client records
- Research property ownership for contact information
- Check client contract for access rights and procedures
- Coordinate with client before attempting access
- Document all access attempts and communications

### **3. In Garage**

#### Purpose:
Target vehicle is inside a closed garage, preventing visual verification or physical access.

#### Description:
The vehicle is believed to be inside a garage (attached, detached, or commercial) but the garage is closed and locked, preventing confirmation and access.

#### Common Scenarios:
- **Attached residential garage** - Part of house, door closed
- **Detached garage** - Separate structure, locked
- **Underground parking** - Secured garage levels
- **Commercial garages** - Business/warehouse storage
- **Shared garages** - Multi-unit building garages
- **Storage units** - Rented garage-style units

#### Why This Matters:
In-garage blocks are:
- **Verification challenges** - Can't confirm vehicle is actually there
- **Access difficult** - Requires keys, opener, or property entry
- **Privacy concerns** - Inside private property
- **Time-consuming** - Must wait for owner to open garage
- **Low success rate** - Often cannot gain access without cooperation

#### Resolution Strategies:
1. **Surveillance** - Watch for garage door opening, confirm vehicle inside
2. **Owner cooperation** - Request garage access (voluntary surrender)
3. **Timing** - Return when owner leaves and garage opens
4. **Side door access** - Some garages have unlocked side doors
5. **Property manager** - Multi-unit buildings may have master access
6. **Legal process** - Court order for property access
7. **Abandon recovery** - Some garage blocks are unresolvable

#### Operational Impact:
In-garage blocks have:
- **Low resolution rate** - 20-40% eventually recoverable
- **Longest timelines** - Weeks to months
- **Highest cost** - May require legal action
- **Lowest priority** - Often not worth continued pursuit

#### Verification Challenges:
- **Cannot confirm** - Vehicle might not actually be in garage
- **Wasted effort** - May pursue access only to find garage empty
- **Client frustration** - Client paying for unconfirmed location
- **Data quality** - Based on assumption, not visual confirmation

#### Best Practices:
- Attempt to verify vehicle is actually in garage (peek through windows, listen for alarm chirp)
- Note garage door type (manual, electric, tilt-up, roll-up)
- Document last time vehicle was seen (if spotting based on history)
- Set realistic client expectations (low recovery probability)
- Consider abandoning pursuit if access unlikely
- Monitor for vehicle movement (may leave garage naturally)

### **4. Blocked by Client**

#### Purpose:
Client has placed a temporary hold on the recovery, requesting that the vehicle not be towed despite being located and accessible.

#### Description:
The vehicle is physically accessible and could be recovered, but the client has instructed your company to pause or delay recovery. This is a business/contractual block, not a physical one.

#### Common Reasons:
- **Payment received** - Debtor paid loan, recovery no longer needed
- **Negotiation in progress** - Client working out payment plan with debtor
- **Bankruptcy filed** - Legal hold during bankruptcy proceedings
- **Client error** - Wrong vehicle or incorrect work order
- **Vehicle sold** - Debtor sold vehicle to third party
- **Insurance claim** - Vehicle involved in insurance matter
- **Legal dispute** - Ownership or lien dispute in progress
- **Client capacity** - Client storage lots full, pause recoveries

#### Why This Matters:
Client-blocked vehicles are:
- **Revenue delayed** - No recovery fee until hold lifted
- **Resource tied up** - Spotter effort expended, no payment
- **Unpredictable duration** - Hold may last days or months
- **Frustrating** - Vehicle accessible but cannot proceed
- **Client relationship** - Must honor client instructions

#### Resolution Strategies:
1. **Regular check-ins** - Contact client weekly for status updates
2. **Document reason** - Record why hold was placed
3. **Timeline request** - Ask client for estimated hold duration
4. **Monitor vehicle** - Ensure it doesn't move during hold period
5. **Hold removal notification** - Get immediate alert when client clears hold
6. **Revalidate location** - Confirm vehicle still at location when hold lifts

#### Operational Impact:
Client blocks have:
- **Variable resolution rate** - 60-80% eventually recovered
- **Unpredictable timelines** - Days to months
- **No additional cost** - Just delayed revenue
- **Requires communication** - Client relationship management

#### Financial Impact:
- **Wasted spotter cost** - Already paid spotter, no revenue yet
- **Opportunity cost** - Spotter could have located different vehicle
- **Delayed cash flow** - Expected revenue postponed
- **Potential cancellation** - Some holds result in work order cancellation

#### Best Practices:
- Document exact reason for client hold with date and contact person
- Set calendar reminder for follow-up
- Request notification when hold is lifted
- Verify vehicle status before attempting recovery after hold
- Track hold reasons to identify patterns (client-specific issues)
- Communicate hold status to drivers to prevent unauthorized recovery

### **5. Blocked by Zone**

#### Purpose:
Company policy prohibits recoveries in this specific geographic zone due to operational, legal, or safety concerns.

#### Description:
The vehicle is located in a zone where your company has decided not to operate due to regulatory restrictions, safety issues, cost concerns, or strategic decisions.

#### Common Reasons:
- **High crime area** - Too dangerous for driver safety
- **Out of service area** - Beyond normal operating radius
- **Permit required** - No operating license for that jurisdiction
- **Unprofitable zone** - Costs exceed revenue potential
- **Contract restriction** - Client agreement excludes certain zones
- **Legal prohibition** - Local ordinances restrict towing operations
- **Access restrictions** - Military base, secure facility, etc.

#### Why This Matters:
Zone blocks are:
- **Policy-driven** - Internal business decision
- **Safety-motivated** - Protect driver wellbeing
- **Strategically sound** - Focus resources on profitable areas
- **Potentially revisable** - Policies can change if justified
- **Client communication needed** - Explain why recovery declined

#### Resolution Strategies:
1. **Policy review** - Evaluate if zone restriction still justified
2. **Special approval** - Request exception for high-value recovery
3. **Partner coordination** - Subcontract to another company in that zone
4. **Client communication** - Explain zone policy, offer alternatives
5. **Pricing adjustment** - Charge premium to offset zone challenges
6. **Risk assessment** - Determine if specific recovery justifies exception

#### Operational Impact:
Zone blocks have:
- **Low resolution rate** - 10-30% (policy rarely changes)
- **Indefinite timeline** - No expected resolution
- **Strategic implications** - May need to expand service area
- **Client dissatisfaction** - Client wants recovery regardless of location

#### Financial Considerations:
- **Opportunity cost** - Potential revenue declined
- **Brand impact** - "Can't do" reputation vs. safety/profitability
- **Expansion evaluation** - If many zone blocks, consider expansion
- **Pricing power** - Rare zones can command premium fees

#### Best Practices:
- Clearly document zone policies and reasons
- Communicate zone restrictions to clients upfront
- Regularly review zone blocks for policy changes
- Track volume of zone-blocked vehicles (opportunity analysis)
- Consider partnerships for out-of-area recoveries
- Exception process for high-value or strategic recoveries

---

### **6. Blocked by Client + Zone**

#### Purpose:
Combination block where client has specific restrictions on certain zones in addition to company zone policies.

#### Description:
The client's contract prohibits recoveries in specific zones, or the combination of client requirements and zone characteristics makes recovery impractical or unprofitable.

#### Common Scenarios:
- **Client doesn't pay enough** - Fee too low for challenging zone
- **Client risk tolerance** - Client prohibits high-crime areas
- **Client capacity** - No storage lot near this zone
- **Client legal limits** - Client's license doesn't cover this jurisdiction
- **Combined restrictions** - Both client and company have zone limits

#### Why This Matters:
Combined blocks are:
- **Doubly restrictive** - Two independent barriers
- **Low priority** - Multiple reasons not to pursue
- **Client relationship** - May indicate scope mismatch
- **Strategic signal** - Client may not be good fit overall

#### Resolution Strategies:
1. **Contract renegotiation** - Expand zone coverage with fee increase
2. **Client storage expansion** - Add storage lot near zone
3. **Subcontracting** - Partner handles these specific recoveries
4. **Service agreement modification** - Formally exclude these zones
5. **Client communication** - Explain impossibility, set expectations

#### Best Practices:
- Document both client and zone restrictions clearly
- Evaluate if client relationship is profitable given restrictions
- Propose solutions during contract renewal
- Track combined blocks to identify systemic issues

---

### **7. Blocked by Client + Zone + Market**

#### Purpose:
Maximum restriction level where client, zone, and market factors combine to make recovery impossible or inadvisable.

#### Description:
Multiple overlapping restrictions create an insurmountable barrier to recovery. Represents vehicles that should not have been assigned or quoted in the first place.

#### Common Scenarios:
- **Out-of-state** - Client doesn't operate, zone uncovered, market not serviced
- **Extreme restrictions** - Client + zone + market all prohibit
- **Operational impossibility** - Combination of factors makes recovery impossible

#### Why This Matters:
Triple blocks are:
- **System failures** - Should not have been accepted as work
- **Waste of resources** - Spotter sent on impossible mission
- **Process improvement opportunity** - Pre-screen work orders better
- **Client education** - Client needs clearer service area definition

#### Resolution Strategies:
1. **Work order screening** - Reject before assigning to spotter
2. **Client service area definition** - Clearly define coverage
3. **Pre-assignment validation** - Check restrictions before accepting work
4. **Client communication** - Explain why work cannot be performed

#### Best Practices:
- Implement automated work order screening
- Define service areas clearly in contracts
- Train dispatch to identify triple-blocked scenarios
- Return unworkable orders to client immediately
- Track to improve work order acceptance process

---

### **8. Other**

#### Purpose:
Catch-all category for blocking reasons that don't fit standard classifications.

#### Common Examples:
- **Vehicle submerged** - In flooded area
- **Heavy snow** - Winter conditions preventing access
- **Construction** - Temporary blocking by construction equipment
- **Accident scene** - Vehicle in crash, police involved
- **Impounded** - Vehicle already towed by police/municipality
- **Vehicle moved** - No longer at spotted location
- **Debtor confrontation** - Safety concern, aggressive owner
- **Alarm issues** - Vehicle alarm prevents silent recovery

#### Best Practices:
- Provide detailed description in notes
- Evaluate if "Other" blocks indicate need for new category
- Review "Other" blocks monthly to identify patterns
- Consider creating new categories if "Other" becomes frequent

## **Status Levels**

### **Active**

#### Purpose:
Block is current, unresolved, and requires attention.

#### Description:
The vehicle is currently blocked, and recovery cannot proceed until the blocking condition is resolved. This is the default status for newly identified blocks.

#### Characteristics:
- **Recently identified** - Block discovered within days
- **No resolution in progress** - No active efforts yet
- **Requires action** - Needs investigation or coordination
- **Priority dependent** - Urgent blocks need immediate attention

#### Operational Actions:
- Investigate resolution options
- Contact relevant parties (property owner, client, etc.)
- Document blocking details thoroughly
- Assign priority level
- Develop resolution plan
- Set expected resolution timeline

---

### **Under Review**

#### Purpose:
Resolution efforts are underway, and the block is being actively worked on.

#### Description:
Someone is actively working to resolve the block - negotiating access, obtaining permissions, coordinating with client, or awaiting response from stakeholders.

#### Characteristics:
- **Action in progress** - Active resolution efforts
- **Awaiting response** - Contacted parties, waiting for callback
- **Coordination underway** - Multiple parties being coordinated
- **Timeline established** - Expected resolution date identified

#### Operational Actions:
- Follow up on pending contacts
- Monitor resolution progress
- Update estimated resolution date
- Escalate if progress stalls
- Document all resolution attempts

---

### **Resolved**

#### Purpose:
Block has been cleared, and vehicle is now available for recovery.

#### Description:
The blocking condition has been removed, and the vehicle can now be recovered. This triggers dispatch to assign a driver.

#### Characteristics:
- **Block cleared** - Fence opened, vehicle moved, hold lifted, etc.
- **Recovery possible** - Can now proceed with tow
- **Time-sensitive** - Should recover quickly before new block occurs
- **Success** - Resolution efforts succeeded

#### Operational Actions:
- Immediately dispatch driver
- Revalidate vehicle location (may have moved)
- Update client on resolution
- Prioritize recovery (block may recur)
- Track resolution method for future reference

---

### **Escalated**

#### Purpose:
Block cannot be resolved through normal channels and requires management intervention, legal action, or client decision.

#### Description:
Standard resolution strategies have failed, and the block requires higher-level attention, additional resources, or strategic decisions about whether to continue pursuit.

#### Characteristics:
- **Standard resolution failed** - Normal approaches unsuccessful
- **Management decision needed** - Pursue vs. abandon
- **Legal involvement possible** - May require court orders
- **High cost implications** - Further effort expensive
- **Client communication critical** - Client must decide next steps

#### Operational Actions:
- Document all resolution attempts and failures
- Present options to management (pursue vs. abandon)
- Involve legal team if appropriate
- Communicate escalation to client
- Determine cost vs. benefit of continued pursuit
- Set decision deadline

#### Escalation Triggers:
- Block persists beyond 30 days
- Multiple resolution attempts failed
- Legal barriers identified
- Cost of resolution exceeds recovery fee
- Safety concerns prevent recovery
- Client relationship at risk

## **Priority Levels**

### **Urgent**

#### Purpose:
Highest priority blocks requiring immediate attention and resources.

#### Characteristics:
- **High-value vehicle** - Expensive car, high recovery fee
- **Time-sensitive** - Vehicle may move soon
- **Client VIP** - Premium client demanding fast resolution
- **Easy resolution** - Block likely to clear quickly if acted on
- **Strategic importance** - Affects key client relationship

#### Service Expectations:
- **Response time**: Same day
- **Resolution efforts**: Multiple attempts daily
- **Management involvement**: Direct oversight
- **Resource allocation**: Best staff assigned
- **Client updates**: Daily communication

---

### **High**

#### Purpose:
Important blocks deserving significant attention and timely resolution efforts.

#### Characteristics:
- **Good recovery fee** - Above-average revenue potential
- **Client priority** - Important but not top-tier client
- **Reasonable resolution chance** - Likely resolvable with effort
- **Standard timeline** - No extreme time pressure

#### Service Expectations:
- **Response time**: Within 2 business days
- **Resolution efforts**: Daily follow-up
- **Management involvement**: Weekly review
- **Resource allocation**: Standard staff assigned
- **Client updates**: Weekly communication

---

### **Medium**

#### Purpose:
Standard priority for typical blocked vehicles with normal service expectations.

#### Characteristics:
- **Standard recovery fee** - Typical pricing
- **Regular client** - Normal service level
- **Moderate resolution complexity** - Standard effort required
- **Normal timeline** - No rush

#### Service Expectations:
- **Response time**: Within 1 week
- **Resolution efforts**: Weekly follow-up
- **Management involvement**: Monthly review
- **Resource allocation**: Assignment when available
- **Client updates**: Bi-weekly communication

---

### **Low**

#### Purpose:
Lowest priority for blocks with minimal revenue potential or low resolution probability.

#### Characteristics:
- **Low recovery fee** - Minimal revenue
- **Difficult block** - Low resolution probability
- **Budget client** - Discount pricing tier
- **Optional recovery** - Fill-in work

#### Service Expectations:
- **Response time**: When convenient
- **Resolution efforts**: Passive monitoring
- **Management involvement**: Quarterly review
- **Resource allocation**: Spare capacity only
- **Client updates**: Upon resolution or abandonment

## **Key Information Fields**

### **Spotted Date**
- **Purpose**: When block was first identified
- **Why It Matters**: Tracks aging of blocked vehicles
- **Action Trigger**: Blocks >30 days old need escalation review

### **Spotted By**
- **Purpose**: Which spotter identified the block
- **Why It Matters**: Accountability and follow-up contact
- **Use Case**: Spotter can provide additional details if needed

### **Estimated Resolution**
- **Purpose**: Expected date when block might clear
- **Why It Matters**: Planning and expectation management
- **Calculation**: Based on blocking reason and resolution efforts

### **Contact Person**
- **Purpose**: Client representative for this vehicle
- **Why It Matters**: Communication and decision-making
- **Use Case**: Updates, approvals, status changes

### **Contact Phone/Email**
- **Purpose**: Communication channels
- **Why It Matters**: Rapid coordination when block resolves
- **Best Practice**: Verify contact info is current

### **Blocked Notes**
- **Purpose**: Detailed description of blocking condition
- **Why It Matters**: Informs resolution strategy
- **Best Practice**: Be specific and detailed

### **Spotter Notes**
- **Purpose**: Field observations from spotter
- **Why It Matters**: On-ground intelligence
- **Includes**: Access details, timing patterns, contact opportunities

### **Access Instructions**
- **Purpose**: How to gain access when block resolves
- **Why It Matters**: Enables quick recovery when cleared
- **Includes**: Gate codes, contact numbers, timing windows

### **Alternative Actions**
- **Purpose**: Possible resolution strategies
- **Why It Matters**: Guides resolution efforts
- **Examples**: Contact owner, legal access, wait for movement

## **Key Performance Metrics**

### **Block Resolution Rate**
- **Formula**: (Resolved Blocks / Total Blocks) × 100
- **Target**: 60-70% overall resolution rate
- **Insights**: Low rate indicates systematic access issues

### **Average Days to Resolution**
- **Formula**: Total days for resolved blocks / Number of resolved blocks
- **Target**: <15 days average
- **Insights**: Long resolution times indicate process inefficiencies

### **Block Rate by Reason**
- **Formula**: (Blocks of type X / Total blocks) × 100
- **Use**: Identify most common blocking reasons
- **Action**: Invest in solutions for most frequent blocks

### **Revenue Lost to Blocks**
- **Formula**: Sum of repo fees for unresolved blocks
- **Use**: Quantify impact of blocking issues
- **Decision**: Justify investment in access solutions

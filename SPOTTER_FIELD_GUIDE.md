# 📋 Spotter Field Guide - Complete Documentation

## Vehicle Intake Form - Field Descriptions

This guide provides detailed descriptions for every field in the Spotter Vehicle Intake Form. Use this as a reference for training spotters and ensuring consistent, high-quality data collection.

---

## 🏢 **Client** *(Required)*

**Purpose**: Identifies which client/company owns or is requesting the vehicle recovery.

**Description**: Select the client organization from the dropdown list. This determines billing, priority level, and specific handling requirements for the vehicle.

**Examples**:
- Santander Consumer USA
- Ally Financial
- Capital One Auto Finance
- Wells Fargo Dealer Services

**Why It Matters**: 
- Different clients have different contract terms
- Affects pricing and service level agreements
- Determines dispatch priority
- Controls access to vehicle information

**Spotter Instructions**: Always verify the client name from the work order or dispatch sheet before selecting.

---

## 🔢 **VIN (Vehicle Identification Number)** *(Required)*

**Purpose**: Uniquely identifies the specific vehicle for legal, tracking, and verification purposes.

**Description**: The 17-character alphanumeric code that serves as the vehicle's fingerprint. This is the most critical piece of information for vehicle recovery.

**Format**: 
- Exactly 17 characters
- Mix of letters and numbers
- No spaces or special characters
- Example: `1HGBH41JXMN109186`

**Where to Find It**:
1. **Dashboard** - Lower left corner of windshield (driver side)
2. **Driver's door jamb** - Sticker with vehicle information
3. **Insurance/registration cards** - If visible in vehicle
4. **Engine block** - For verification (rarely accessible)

**Why It Matters**:
- Legal proof of vehicle identity
- Prevents wrong vehicle recovery (costly mistake)
- Required for all DMV and title work
- Used to verify ownership and lien holder
- Critical for insurance and liability

**Spotter Instructions**: 
- Take a clear photo of the VIN plate through the windshield
- Double-check all 17 characters for accuracy
- If VIN is not visible, note in comments and contact dispatch
- Never guess or estimate the VIN

**Common Mistakes to Avoid**:
- Confusing the letter "O" with the number "0"
- Confusing the letter "I" with the number "1"
- Missing or adding characters
- Reading from wrong location (some stickers show partial VINs)

## 🚗 **Plate (License Plate Number)** *(Required)*

**Purpose**: Provides a quick visual identifier for the vehicle and confirms registration status.

**Description**: The state-issued license plate number currently displayed on the vehicle. This helps with quick identification and verification.

**Format**:
- Varies by state
- May include letters, numbers, or both
- Include state abbreviation if possible
- Example: `MD ABC1234` or `VA XYZ-5678`

**Where to Find It**:
- **Rear plate** - Back of vehicle (primary)
- **Front plate** - Front of vehicle (if state requires)
- **Temporary tags** - Paper tag in rear window

**Why It Matters**:
- Quick visual verification for drivers
- Helps confirm correct vehicle before pickup
- Used for police reports if needed
- Assists with vehicle location in crowded areas
- Required for some state regulations

**Spotter Instructions**:
- Record exactly as shown (include dashes, spaces)
- Note if plate is missing, damaged, or temporary
- Take a photo of the plate for verification
- If no plate, write "NO PLATE" and note in comments

**Special Cases**:
- **Temporary tags**: Note expiration date in comments
- **Dealer plates**: Note dealer name
- **Out-of-state plates**: Include state abbreviation
- **Missing plates**: Document and notify dispatch immediately

## 📅 **Year** *(Required)*

**Purpose**: Identifies the model year of the vehicle for accurate identification and value assessment.

**Description**: The manufacturing year of the vehicle, typically found on the door jamb sticker or derived from the VIN.

**Format**:
- 4-digit year
- Range: Typically 1980-2025
- Example: `2019`, `2023`

**Where to Find It**:
1. **Door jamb sticker** - Most reliable source
2. **VIN decoder** - 10th character indicates year
3. **Visual estimation** - Based on body style (least reliable)

**Why It Matters**:
- Affects vehicle value and recovery priority
- Determines towing method (older vehicles may need special handling)
- Impacts parts availability
- Used for insurance valuation
- Helps verify correct vehicle

**Spotter Instructions**:
- Use door jamb sticker when possible
- If unsure, estimate conservatively and note in comments
- Don't confuse model year with current year
- Newer vehicles (2020+) may have different security features

**VIN Year Code Reference** (10th character):
- A = 2010, B = 2011, C = 2012, D = 2013, E = 2014
- F = 2015, G = 2016, H = 2017, J = 2018, K = 2019
- L = 2020, M = 2021, N = 2022, P = 2023, R = 2024

## 🏭 **Make** *(Required)*

**Purpose**: Identifies the vehicle manufacturer for proper handling and equipment needs.

**Description**: The brand or manufacturer of the vehicle (e.g., Ford, Toyota, Honda).

**Format**:
- Full manufacturer name
- Use standard spelling
- Example: `Toyota` (not "Toyoda"), `Chevrolet` (not "Chevy")

**Common Makes**:
- **Domestic**: Ford, Chevrolet, Dodge, GMC, Jeep, Ram, Cadillac, Lincoln
- **Asian**: Toyota, Honda, Nissan, Hyundai, Kia, Mazda, Subaru, Mitsubishi
- **European**: BMW, Mercedes-Benz, Volkswagen, Audi, Volvo, Land Rover
- **Luxury**: Lexus, Acura, Infiniti, Genesis, Porsche, Jaguar

**Why It Matters**:
- Determines towing equipment needed
- Affects vehicle weight and size
- Some makes require special handling (AWD, low clearance)
- Impacts recovery difficulty and time
- Used for parts and key codes

**Spotter Instructions**:
- Use full official name (not nicknames)
- Check emblems on front grille, rear, and steering wheel
- If unsure, take a photo of the emblem
- Note if emblems have been removed or modified

**Special Considerations**:
- **Luxury vehicles**: May have enhanced security systems
- **Electric vehicles**: Require special towing procedures
- **AWD/4WD vehicles**: Need flatbed or dollies
- **European vehicles**: May have steering wheel locks

## 🚙 **Model** *(Required)*

**Purpose**: Specifies the exact vehicle model for accurate identification and handling.

**Description**: The specific model name or designation within the manufacturer's lineup (e.g., Camry, F-150, Civic).

**Format**:
- Official model name
- Include trim level if visible (e.g., "Accord EX", "F-150 Lariat")
- Example: `Camry`, `Silverado 1500`, `Civic Si`

**Where to Find It**:
- **Rear emblems** - Most common location
- **Door jamb sticker** - Official designation
- **Owner's manual** - If visible in vehicle
- **Body style** - Visual identification

**Why It Matters**:
- Confirms exact vehicle specification
- Determines vehicle size and weight class
- Affects towing method (truck vs. sedan)
- Helps verify against work order
- Impacts recovery complexity

**Spotter Instructions**:
- Record model name from rear emblem
- Include trim level if clearly marked
- Note if emblems are missing or modified
- Use generic terms if specific model unclear (e.g., "Sedan", "Pickup")

**Common Model Categories**:
- **Sedans**: Camry, Accord, Altima, Malibu
- **SUVs**: CR-V, RAV4, Explorer, Tahoe
- **Trucks**: F-150, Silverado, Ram 1500, Tacoma
- **Compacts**: Civic, Corolla, Sentra, Cruze

## 🎨 **Color** *(Required)*

**Purpose**: Provides a quick visual identifier for locating and verifying the vehicle.

**Description**: The primary exterior color of the vehicle. Use simple, standard color names.

**Standard Colors**:
- **Basic**: Black, White, Silver, Gray, Red, Blue, Green, Brown, Gold, Beige
- **Variations**: Dark Blue, Light Blue, Navy, Burgundy, Maroon
- **Special**: Two-Tone (specify both colors)

**Format**:
- Use common color names
- Avoid creative descriptions
- Example: `Silver` (not "Metallic Moonstone Silver")

**Why It Matters**:
- Primary visual identifier for drivers
- Helps locate vehicle in crowded areas
- Quick verification before pickup
- Reduces wrong vehicle pickups
- Assists police if needed

**Spotter Instructions**:
- Use the dominant color if multi-tone
- Note if heavily faded or damaged
- Mention if wrapped or painted differently
- Take a photo showing true color

**Special Cases**:
- **Two-tone**: List both colors (e.g., "Black/Silver")
- **Faded paint**: Note original color + "faded"
- **Primer/unpainted**: Note as "Primer" or "Unpainted"
- **Custom colors**: Use closest standard color

## 📍 **Address** *(Required)*

**Purpose**: Provides the exact location where the vehicle is currently parked for driver navigation.

**Description**: The complete street address where the vehicle is located. This must be as accurate as possible for efficient recovery.

**Format**:
- Full street address with number
- Include apartment/unit number if applicable
- City, State, ZIP code
- Example: `1234 Main Street, Apt 5B, Baltimore, MD 21201`

**Components**:
1. **Street number** - Building/house number
2. **Street name** - Full street name
3. **Unit/Apt** - If applicable
4. **City** - Full city name
5. **State** - Two-letter abbreviation
6. **ZIP** - 5-digit postal code

**Why It Matters**:
- Enables driver GPS navigation
- Determines zone assignment
- Affects recovery time estimates
- Used for route optimization
- Required for legal documentation

**Spotter Instructions**:
- Verify address with street signs
- Use GPS coordinates if address unclear
- Note landmarks for difficult-to-find locations
- Include cross streets in comments if helpful
- Verify with mapping app before submitting

**Special Locations**:
- **Apartment complexes**: Include building and unit number
- **Shopping centers**: Note specific store or lot section
- **Office parks**: Include building name/number
- **Gated communities**: Note gate code in secure comments
- **Rural areas**: Provide detailed directions in comments

**GPS Coordinates**:
- If address is unclear, provide lat/long
- Format: `39.2904° N, 76.6122° W`
- Most mapping apps show coordinates

## ✅ **Reachable** *(Required)*

**Purpose**: Indicates whether the tow truck can physically access the vehicle for immediate recovery.

**Description**: A yes/no toggle that tells the driver if they can drive up to the vehicle and hook it up without obstacles.

**Options**:
- **✅ Reachable** - Truck can access the vehicle directly
- **❌ Not Reachable** - Physical obstacles prevent direct access

**"Reachable" Means**:
- Tow truck can park within 10-15 feet of vehicle
- Clear path to hook up towing equipment
- No gates, fences, or barriers blocking access
- No need for special equipment or permissions
- Can be recovered in single trip

**"Not Reachable" Means**:
- Behind locked gate or fence
- In garage or carport
- Blocked by other vehicles
- On private property requiring permission
- Needs dollies or special equipment
- Requires coordination with property owner

**Why It Matters**:
- Determines if vehicle can be recovered immediately
- Affects driver route planning
- Impacts time estimates and scheduling
- May require additional equipment or personnel
- Influences priority and assignment

**Spotter Instructions**:
- Walk around the vehicle to assess access
- Consider tow truck size (large vehicles need more space)
- Note if access is time-dependent (e.g., gate opens at 8 AM)
- Mark "Not Reachable" if ANY obstacle exists
- Provide detailed access notes in comments

**Common "Not Reachable" Scenarios**:
1. **Gated communities** - Need access code or resident
2. **Private garages** - Need key or owner permission
3. **Blocked driveways** - Other vehicles in the way
4. **Tight alleys** - Truck cannot fit
5. **Underground parking** - Low clearance
6. **Locked compounds** - Commercial/industrial lots

**Access Notes to Include**:
- Gate codes or access instructions
- Best time for access (e.g., "Gate opens 6 AM-10 PM")
- Contact person for access
- Alternative entry points
- Special equipment needed

## 🔧 **Rusted** *(Required)*

**Purpose**: Alerts the driver to potential structural issues that may affect towing safety and vehicle condition.

**Description**: Indicates whether the vehicle has significant rust that could impact recovery or cause liability issues.

**Options**:
- **❌ Not Rusted** - Normal wear, no structural rust
- **⚠️ Rusted** - Visible rust that may affect towing or safety

**"Rusted" Means**:
- Visible rust on frame, body, or structural components
- Rust holes or perforations
- Weakened tow points or frame
- Potential for parts to break during recovery
- May need extra care during towing

**"Not Rusted" Means**:
- Normal surface rust (common on older vehicles)
- Minor cosmetic rust on body panels
- Rust-free tow points and frame
- Safe for standard towing procedures

**Why It Matters**:
- **Safety**: Rusted tow points may break during recovery
- **Liability**: Prevents damage claims from pre-existing rust
- **Equipment**: May need special towing methods
- **Documentation**: Protects company from rust-related damage claims
- **Driver preparation**: Alerts driver to inspect before hooking up

**Spotter Instructions**:
- Inspect tow points (front and rear)
- Check frame rails and undercarriage (if visible)
- Look for rust holes or perforations
- Note if rust is cosmetic vs. structural
- Take photos of any significant rust
- When in doubt, mark as "Rusted" and photograph

**Areas to Inspect**:
1. **Tow points** - Front and rear hooks/loops
2. **Frame rails** - Undercarriage structural members
3. **Rocker panels** - Lower body sides
4. **Wheel wells** - Behind wheels
5. **Trunk/hatch floor** - Water damage areas
6. **Door bottoms** - Common rust location

**Rust Severity Levels** (note in comments):
- **Minor**: Surface rust, cosmetic only
- **Moderate**: Rust through paint, some pitting
- **Severe**: Rust holes, structural weakness, flaking metal

**Photo Requirements**:
- If marked "Rusted", include photos showing:
  - Tow point condition
  - Frame rust (if visible)
  - Any holes or perforations
  - Overall rust severity

## 🏠 **Location Type** *(Required)*

**Purpose**: Describes the type of property where the vehicle is located to help drivers prepare for site-specific challenges.

**Description**: Categorizes the location to set expectations for access, parking, and recovery complexity.

**Options**:

### **1. Single Family Home**
- **Description**: Standalone house with private driveway
- **Typical Access**: Direct driveway or street parking
- **Challenges**: May need homeowner permission, dogs, narrow driveways
- **Best Time**: Daytime to avoid disturbing residents

### **2. Apartment Complex**
- **Description**: Multi-unit residential building with shared parking
- **Typical Access**: Parking lot or assigned spaces
- **Challenges**: Gated entry, parking permits, tight spaces, resident complaints
- **Best Time**: Early morning or late evening (less traffic)

### **3. Townhouse/Condo**
- **Description**: Attached housing with HOA or shared amenities
- **Typical Access**: Shared driveways or parking areas
- **Challenges**: HOA rules, narrow streets, visitor parking only
- **Best Time**: Weekday mornings (residents at work)

### **4. Commercial Property**
- **Description**: Business location (store, office, warehouse)
- **Typical Access**: Parking lot or loading area
- **Challenges**: Business hours restrictions, security, employee vehicles
- **Best Time**: After business hours or early morning

### **5. Street Parking**
- **Description**: Public street or roadside
- **Typical Access**: Direct from street
- **Challenges**: Traffic, parking restrictions, permits, narrow streets
- **Best Time**: Low-traffic hours (avoid rush hour)

### **6. Shopping Center/Mall**
- **Description**: Retail complex with large parking lot
- **Typical Access**: Open parking lot
- **Challenges**: Security patrols, busy during business hours, cart returns
- **Best Time**: Early morning before stores open

### **7. Industrial/Warehouse**
- **Description**: Commercial/industrial facility
- **Typical Access**: Gated compound or loading dock
- **Challenges**: Security checkpoints, restricted access, business hours
- **Best Time**: Coordinate with facility manager

### **8. Public Parking Lot/Garage**
- **Description**: Paid or public parking facility
- **Typical Access**: Parking structure or lot
- **Challenges**: Height restrictions, tight turns, payment required, security
- **Best Time**: Off-peak hours

### **9. Rural/Farm Property**
- **Description**: Country location, unpaved roads
- **Typical Access**: Long driveways, dirt roads
- **Challenges**: Soft ground, dogs, property owner, distance from road
- **Best Time**: Daylight hours, dry weather

### **10. Other**
- **Description**: Unusual location not fitting above categories
- **Examples**: Abandoned property, impound lot, repair shop, dealership
- **Instructions**: Provide detailed description in comments

**Why It Matters**:
- Helps driver prepare mentally and physically
- Determines equipment needs (e.g., dollies for tight spaces)
- Affects time estimates
- Identifies potential access issues
- Helps with route planning and scheduling

**Spotter Instructions**:
- Choose the most specific category
- Note any unique features in comments
- Consider access challenges for large tow trucks
- Mention if location type affects best recovery time

## 🅿️ **Parked** *(Required)*

**Purpose**: Describes how the vehicle is positioned to help the driver plan the towing approach.

**Description**: Indicates the vehicle's parking orientation, which affects hook-up method and recovery complexity.

**Options**:

### **1. Pulled In (Front First)**
- **Description**: Vehicle nose is facing the building/wall
- **Visual**: 🚗 ➡️ 🏢
- **Towing Method**: Must back out or use dollies
- **Difficulty**: Moderate (requires backing out or front dolly)
- **Time**: +5-10 minutes

### **2. Backed In (Rear First)**
- **Description**: Vehicle rear is facing the building/wall
- **Visual**: 🏢 ⬅️ 🚗
- **Towing Method**: Can hook front and pull straight out
- **Difficulty**: Easy (ideal for towing)
- **Time**: Standard recovery time

### **3. Parallel (Street Parking)**
- **Description**: Vehicle parked parallel to curb
- **Visual**: 🚗 ║ ║ ║ curb
- **Towing Method**: Pull forward or back to clear space
- **Difficulty**: Easy to moderate (depends on space)
- **Time**: Standard, may need to maneuver

### **4. Angled**
- **Description**: Vehicle parked at an angle (diagonal parking)
- **Visual**: 🚗 ↗️
- **Towing Method**: Straighten out then hook
- **Difficulty**: Easy
- **Time**: Standard

### **5. Blocked**
- **Description**: Other vehicles or obstacles prevent direct access
- **Visual**: 🚗 🚗 [TARGET] 🚗 🚗
- **Towing Method**: May need to move other vehicles or wait
- **Difficulty**: Hard (may require coordination)
- **Time**: +15-30 minutes or return later

### **6. Sideways/Perpendicular**
- **Description**: Vehicle parked across multiple spaces or at odd angle
- **Visual**: 🚗 (rotated 90°)
- **Towing Method**: Reposition before hooking
- **Difficulty**: Moderate
- **Time**: +5-10 minutes

**Why It Matters**:
- Determines hook-up approach (front vs. rear)
- Affects time estimates
- Identifies need for dollies or special equipment
- Helps driver visualize the recovery
- Influences driver assignment (skill level)

**Spotter Instructions**:
- Stand behind the vehicle and assess orientation
- Note if vehicle can be pulled straight out
- Mention if repositioning is needed
- Consider space available for maneuvering
- Take a photo showing parking position

**Additional Parking Notes to Include**:
- Distance from curb or wall
- Space available in front/behind
- Obstacles nearby (poles, carts, trees)
- Slope or incline
- Surface type (paved, gravel, grass)

## 📝 **Notes (Checkboxes)**

**Purpose**: Quickly flags common vehicle conditions that affect recovery, safety, or value.

**Description**: Pre-defined checkboxes for frequently encountered vehicle issues. Check all that apply.

### **☑️ Tires Deflated**
- **Meaning**: One or more tires are flat or significantly under-inflated
- **Impact**: May need dollies or flatbed, cannot be towed on deflated tires
- **Driver Action**: Bring dollies or plan for flatbed
- **Safety**: Towing on flat tires damages rims and is unsafe
- **Time Impact**: +10-15 minutes for dolly setup

**When to Check**:
- Any tire visibly flat or touching rim
- Multiple tires low on air
- Sidewall damage or punctures

### **☑️ Electronic Brake**
- **Meaning**: Vehicle has electronic parking brake engaged
- **Impact**: Cannot be released without power, may need special procedures
- **Driver Action**: May need jump box or key to release
- **Common On**: Newer vehicles (2015+), especially luxury brands
- **Time Impact**: +5-10 minutes to release

**When to Check**:
- Parking brake light visible on dash
- Rear wheels locked (won't roll)
- Known electronic brake models (check make/model)

**Common Vehicles with Electronic Brakes**:
- Most BMW, Mercedes, Audi (2010+)
- Honda Accord (2018+)
- Toyota Camry (2018+)
- Tesla (all models)
- Many luxury and electric vehicles

### **☑️ Mechanical Issue**
- **Meaning**: Visible mechanical problems beyond normal wear
- **Impact**: May affect towing method or require special handling
- **Driver Action**: Inspect before hooking up, may need flatbed
- **Examples**: Broken suspension, damaged steering, transmission issues
- **Time Impact**: Varies based on severity

**When to Check**:
- Visible suspension damage (sagging, broken springs)
- Steering components hanging or broken
- Transmission fluid leaking
- Drive shaft disconnected
- Engine components hanging

**Note in Comments**: Describe the specific mechanical issue

### **☑️ Cosmetic Issue**
- **Meaning**: Visible body damage, dents, scratches, or missing parts
- **Impact**: Document pre-existing damage to avoid liability
- **Driver Action**: Inspect and photograph before towing
- **Examples**: Dents, scratches, broken lights, missing bumpers
- **Time Impact**: +2-5 minutes for documentation

**When to Check**:
- Any dents, scratches, or body damage
- Broken or missing lights
- Cracked windshield or windows
- Missing trim or body parts
- Paint damage or fading

**Importance**: Protects company from false damage claims

## 💬 **Additional Notes (Text Field)**

**Purpose**: Provides space for any information that doesn't fit in the structured fields above.

**Description**: Free-form text area for detailed observations, special instructions, or unique circumstances.

**What to Include**:

### **Access Details**
- Gate codes or entry instructions
- Best time for recovery
- Contact person for access
- Security procedures
- Parking restrictions

**Example**: "Gate code #1234. Security guard on duty 6 PM-6 AM. Must check in at office first."

### **Vehicle Condition Details**
- Specific damage descriptions
- Unusual modifications
- Missing parts inventory
- Interior condition (if visible)
- Odor issues (smoke, mold)

**Example**: "Heavy front-end damage. Hood won't close. Airbags deployed. Strong smoke smell."

### **Location Challenges**
- Narrow streets or tight spaces
- Steep hills or slopes
- Soft ground or mud
- Traffic patterns
- Parking restrictions

**Example**: "Very narrow alley. Truck must enter from Oak St. Avoid during rush hour (7-9 AM)."

### **Safety Concerns**
- Aggressive dogs
- Hostile property owner
- Unsafe neighborhood
- Structural hazards
- Weather considerations

**Example**: "Large dog in yard. Owner aware of recovery. Bring police escort if needed."

### **Special Instructions**
- Client-specific requirements
- Priority level
- Delivery instructions
- Key location (if available)
- Alarm information

**Example**: "Priority recovery. Client wants photos of odometer and VIN before towing. Deliver to Baltimore yard by 5 PM."

### **Verification Issues**
- VIN not visible
- Plate missing or damaged
- Conflicting information
- Uncertainty about details

**Example**: "VIN plate obscured by sun shade. Verified last 6 digits through window. Full VIN needed before dispatch."

**Spotter Instructions**:
- Be specific and detailed
- Use clear, professional language
- Include anything that would help the driver
- Note any conversations with property owners
- Mention if follow-up needed

**Character Limit**: Typically 500-1000 characters (varies by system)

## 📸 **Photos** *(Required - Max 5)*

**Purpose**: Provides visual verification of vehicle identity, condition, and location for drivers and documentation.

**Description**: Upload 1-5 photos showing key aspects of the vehicle and its surroundings.

**Requirements**:
- **Minimum**: 1 photo required
- **Maximum**: 5 photos allowed
- **File Types**: JPG, PNG, or HEIC
- **File Size**: Up to 10MB each
- **Total**: Up to 5 photos per submission

### **Required Photos** (Priority Order):

#### **1. VIN Plate (CRITICAL - Always Required)**
- **Location**: Dashboard, driver side, visible through windshield
- **Angle**: Straight-on to avoid glare
- **Content**: All 17 characters must be clearly readable
- **Lighting**: Avoid glare, shadows, or reflections
- **Why**: Legal proof of vehicle identity

**Tips**:
- Shoot from outside windshield
- Avoid direct sunlight glare
- Use flash if VIN is in shadow
- Zoom in to fill frame with VIN plate
- Ensure all characters are in focus

#### **2. Full Vehicle (Front 3/4 Angle)**
- **Location**: Front corner showing front and side
- **Angle**: 45-degree angle from front corner
- **Content**: Entire vehicle, license plate, surrounding area
- **Context**: Show parking position and nearby obstacles
- **Why**: Overall condition and location verification

**Tips**:
- Stand 10-15 feet back
- Capture entire vehicle in frame
- Show surrounding context (other cars, buildings)
- Include license plate in shot
- Show parking orientation

#### **3. License Plate (Close-Up)**
- **Location**: Rear plate (or front if no rear)
- **Angle**: Straight-on
- **Content**: Plate number, state, expiration (if visible)
- **Lighting**: Avoid glare or shadows
- **Why**: Quick visual verification for drivers

**Tips**:
- Fill frame with plate
- Ensure all characters are readable
- Capture plate condition (damaged, missing, temp tag)
- Include state name/abbreviation

#### **4. Damage Documentation (If Applicable)**
- **Location**: Any visible damage areas
- **Angle**: Close-up of specific damage
- **Content**: Dents, scratches, rust, broken parts
- **Context**: Show severity and location on vehicle
- **Why**: Protects company from false damage claims

**Tips**:
- One photo per damage area (if multiple)
- Close enough to see detail
- Far enough to show context
- Capture rust, dents, missing parts
- Document pre-existing damage

#### **5. Location/Access (If Challenging)**
- **Location**: Surrounding area showing access
- **Angle**: Driver's approach perspective
- **Content**: Obstacles, gates, narrow spaces, parking context
- **Context**: Show what driver will encounter
- **Why**: Helps driver prepare and plan approach

**Tips**:
- Show gate codes or entry points
- Capture narrow alleys or tight spaces
- Show obstacles (other cars, poles, walls)
- Include landmarks for navigation
- Show parking lot section or building numbers

### **Photo Quality Guidelines**:

**✅ Good Photos**:
- Clear and in focus
- Well-lit (natural light or flash)
- Correct orientation (not sideways)
- Shows required details
- No obstructions blocking view

**❌ Poor Photos**:
- Blurry or out of focus
- Too dark or too bright (overexposed)
- Glare obscuring details
- Too far away (can't read text)
- Sideways or upside down

### **Technical Requirements**:

**File Formats**:
- **JPG/JPEG** - Most common, universally compatible
- **PNG** - Higher quality, larger file size
- **HEIC** - iPhone default (automatically converted)

**File Size**:
- Maximum 10MB per photo
- Typical phone photo: 2-5MB
- If too large, phone will auto-compress

**Resolution**:
- Minimum: 1280x720 pixels (720p)
- Recommended: 1920x1080 pixels (1080p)
- Maximum: 4K (not necessary, creates large files)

### **Photo Upload Process**:

1. **Tap "Upload Vehicle Photos"** button
2. **Choose source**:
   - Take new photo with camera
   - Select from photo library
3. **Select up to 5 photos**
4. **Review thumbnails** before submitting
5. **Delete and retake** if photo is unclear
6. **Submit** when satisfied with all photos

### **Common Photo Mistakes**:

❌ **VIN not readable** - Glare, blur, or too far away
❌ **Plate obscured** - Finger, shadow, or glare
❌ **Vehicle cut off** - Too close, missing parts of car
❌ **Wrong vehicle** - Photographed neighbor's car
❌ **No context** - Can't tell where vehicle is located
❌ **Dark/blurry** - Shot at night without flash
❌ **Sideways orientation** - Phone held wrong way

### **Why Photos Matter**:

1. **Legal Protection**: VIN photo proves correct vehicle
2. **Damage Documentation**: Protects from false claims
3. **Driver Preparation**: Shows what to expect
4. **Verification**: Confirms vehicle matches work order
5. **Evidence**: Provides proof of condition and location
6. **Efficiency**: Reduces callbacks and confusion
7. **Quality Control**: Ensures accurate data collection

### **Photo Best Practices**:

✅ **Take photos in daylight** when possible
✅ **Clean windshield** before VIN photo
✅ **Stand back** for full vehicle shots
✅ **Get close** for VIN and plate
✅ **Check focus** before moving on
✅ **Retake** if any doubt about quality
✅ **Capture context** showing location
✅ **Document damage** to protect company

## 📱 **Spotter Workflow Summary**

1. **Arrive at location** - Verify address
2. **Locate vehicle** - Confirm it matches work order
3. **Photograph VIN** - Through windshield, all 17 characters
4. **Walk around vehicle** - Assess condition and access
5. **Fill out form** - Complete all required fields
6. **Take photos** - VIN, plate, full vehicle, damage, location
7. **Add notes** - Any special instructions or concerns
8. **Review submission** - Double-check VIN and address
9. **Submit** - Send to dispatch for driver assignment

## ⚠️ **Common Mistakes to Avoid**

1. **Wrong VIN** - Most costly mistake, leads to wrong vehicle recovery
2. **Inaccurate address** - Driver goes to wrong location
3. **Missing photos** - Delays dispatch, requires follow-up
4. **Incomplete notes** - Driver unprepared for challenges
5. **Wrong "Reachable" status** - Driver arrives without proper equipment
6. **Not documenting damage** - Company liable for pre-existing damage
7. **Poor photo quality** - Can't verify VIN or condition
8. **Guessing information** - Always verify or note uncertainty

## 📞 **When to Contact Dispatch**

- **VIN not visible** - Cannot see through windshield
- **Address unclear** - GPS shows different location
- **Safety concerns** - Aggressive dogs, hostile owner
- **Access issues** - Cannot determine if reachable
- **Wrong vehicle** - Doesn't match work order description
- **Unusual circumstances** - Anything not covered by form

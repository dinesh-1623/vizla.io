# Spotter Field Guide - Detailed Documentation

## Vehicle Intake Form - Complete Field Descriptions

This document provides comprehensive explanations for each field in the Spotter Vehicle Intake Form, including the purpose, importance, and impact on operations.

---

## **Client** *(Required)*

### Purpose:
The Client field identifies which financial institution, bank, or company owns the vehicle or has contracted for its recovery. This is the organization that will be billed for the towing service and has specific requirements for how their vehicles should be handled.

### Why This Matters:
Different clients have different contractual agreements with your company. Each client may have unique pricing structures, service level agreements, priority levels, and specific handling instructions. For example, some clients require photos of the odometer, others need vehicles delivered to specific storage lots, and some have expedited recovery requirements. The client selection also determines:

- **Billing rates and invoicing procedures** - Each client has negotiated rates
- **Priority level** - Some clients pay premium rates for faster service
- **Handling requirements** - Special instructions for vehicle condition documentation
- **Delivery locations** - Which storage lot to use
- **Reporting requirements** - What information must be documented
- **Legal compliance** - Client-specific regulatory requirements

### Impact on Operations:
When a driver receives the assignment, they immediately know which procedures to follow based on the client. This prevents confusion, ensures compliance with contracts, and maintains positive client relationships. Incorrect client selection can result in billing errors, contract violations, and potential loss of business.

## **VIN (Vehicle Identification Number)** *(Required)*

### Purpose:
The VIN is a unique 17-character alphanumeric code assigned to every vehicle manufactured since 1981. It serves as the vehicle's fingerprint and is the single most important piece of information for legal identification and verification. No two vehicles in the world share the same VIN.

### Why This Matters:
The VIN is legally binding proof that you have recovered the correct vehicle. It is used by:

- **Law enforcement** - To verify the vehicle is not stolen
- **DMV and title companies** - For ownership transfers and registration
- **Insurance companies** - For claims and coverage verification
- **Lien holders** - To confirm they have the right to repossess
- **Courts** - As legal evidence in disputes
- **Your company** - To protect against wrongful recovery lawsuits

Recovering the wrong vehicle is one of the most serious and costly mistakes in the towing industry. It can result in:
- Criminal charges (theft, trespassing)
- Civil lawsuits from vehicle owners
- Loss of business licenses and contracts
- Damage to company reputation
- Financial liability for damages

### Impact on Operations:
Every driver must verify the VIN matches the work order before hooking up the vehicle. The spotter's accurate VIN documentation is the first line of defense against wrong vehicle recovery. A clear photo of the VIN plate allows dispatch to verify the vehicle before assigning a driver, and the driver can double-check on-site before towing. This single field prevents potentially catastrophic legal and financial consequences.

---

## **Plate (License Plate Number)** *(Required)*

### Purpose:
The license plate number is the state-issued registration identifier displayed on the vehicle. While not as legally definitive as the VIN, it provides a quick and easy way for drivers to visually identify the correct vehicle, especially when multiple similar vehicles are in the same area.

### Why This Matters:
License plates serve as the primary visual identifier that drivers use when approaching a vehicle. When a driver arrives at a location with multiple cars, they first look for the plate number to quickly locate the target vehicle. The plate also:

- **Confirms vehicle registration status** - Active, expired, or temporary
- **Provides state of registration** - Important for interstate recoveries
- **Assists law enforcement** - If police involvement is needed
- **Helps with parking enforcement** - If vehicle is in violation
- **Aids in vehicle location** - Easier to spot than reading VINs
- **Verifies work order accuracy** - Cross-reference with client information

### Impact on Operations:
A clearly documented plate number saves drivers significant time when locating vehicles in crowded parking lots, apartment complexes, or street parking situations. It also serves as a secondary verification point - if the plate doesn't match the work order, the driver knows to stop and verify the VIN before proceeding. Missing or incorrect plate information can lead to delays, confusion, and potential wrong vehicle recovery.

---

## **Year** *(Required)*

### Purpose:
The vehicle's model year indicates when it was manufactured and represents its generation within the manufacturer's production timeline. This information helps verify vehicle identity and provides critical context for recovery operations.

### Why This Matters:
The model year affects multiple aspects of vehicle recovery:

- **Vehicle value** - Newer vehicles are worth more and require extra care
- **Security systems** - Newer vehicles (2015+) often have electronic parking brakes, advanced alarms, and immobilizers that affect towing procedures
- **Towing methods** - Older vehicles may have rust or structural issues requiring different handling
- **Parts and keys** - Year determines key type and availability
- **Weight and size** - Vehicles have grown larger over time, affecting equipment needs
- **Technology** - Newer vehicles may have cameras, sensors, or systems that need special attention
- **Recovery complexity** - Modern vehicles with electronic systems require different approaches than older mechanical vehicles

### Impact on Operations:
When a driver sees a 2023 vehicle on the work order, they know to expect electronic parking brakes, backup cameras, and potentially more sophisticated security systems. A 2005 vehicle suggests simpler systems but possible rust or mechanical issues. This advance knowledge helps drivers bring the right tools and plan their approach. The year also helps verify vehicle identity - if the work order says 2020 but the vehicle looks like it's from 2005, the driver knows to stop and verify before proceeding.

## **Make** *(Required)*

### Purpose:
The make identifies the vehicle's manufacturer or brand (e.g., Ford, Toyota, Honda, BMW). This tells the driver which company built the vehicle and indicates the general category of vehicle they'll be recovering.

### Why This Matters:
Different manufacturers have different engineering approaches, which directly affects recovery operations:

- **Towing requirements** - Subaru and Audi (AWD systems) require flatbed or dollies; front-wheel-drive vehicles can be towed from the front
- **Security systems** - European luxury brands (BMW, Mercedes, Audi) have more sophisticated alarms and electronic parking brakes
- **Weight distribution** - American trucks are heavier; Asian sedans are lighter
- **Key systems** - Some makes use proximity keys, others use traditional keys
- **Special procedures** - Tesla and other EVs require specific towing methods to avoid damaging electric motors
- **Parts availability** - Common makes (Ford, Toyota) have readily available parts; exotic brands may need special handling
- **Value considerations** - Luxury brands require extra care to avoid damage

### Impact on Operations:
When a driver sees "Subaru" on the work order, they immediately know they need dollies or a flatbed because of the AWD system. "BMW" tells them to expect an electronic parking brake. "Ford F-150" indicates a large, heavy truck requiring different equipment than a Honda Civic. This advance knowledge allows drivers to bring appropriate equipment and plan their time accordingly. The make also helps with visual identification - drivers can quickly scan a parking lot for the manufacturer's distinctive styling.

---

## **Model** *(Required)*

### Purpose:
The model specifies the exact vehicle line within the manufacturer's product range (e.g., Camry, F-150, Civic, Silverado). This provides precise identification and indicates the vehicle's size, type, and specific characteristics.

### Why This Matters:
The model name tells the driver exactly what type of vehicle they're recovering:

- **Vehicle size** - A Ford F-350 is much larger than an F-150
- **Vehicle type** - Sedan, SUV, truck, van, or sports car
- **Weight class** - Affects towing capacity requirements
- **Ground clearance** - Low sports cars need extra care; high trucks are easier
- **Wheelbase** - Longer vehicles need more space to maneuver
- **Special features** - Some models have unique characteristics (Corvette's low clearance, Wrangler's removable doors)
- **Value tier** - Base model vs. luxury trim affects care requirements
- **Recovery complexity** - Sports cars and luxury vehicles need extra attention

### Impact on Operations:
The model name helps drivers visualize exactly what they'll be towing before they arrive. "Corvette" means low clearance and expensive - extra care needed. "Suburban" means large, heavy SUV - plan for longer recovery time. "Prius" means hybrid with electronic systems. This specificity allows for better time estimates, appropriate equipment selection, and mental preparation for the recovery. It also aids in vehicle identification - if the work order says "Mustang" but the vehicle on-site is a sedan, the driver knows something is wrong.

## **Color** *(Required)*

### Purpose:
The color field documents the vehicle's primary exterior paint color using simple, standard color names. This is the most immediate visual characteristic that helps drivers quickly locate and identify the correct vehicle.

### Why This Matters:
Color is the first thing drivers look for when approaching a location:

- **Quick visual identification** - Easiest way to spot the vehicle from a distance
- **Distinguishes similar vehicles** - When multiple same-make vehicles are present
- **Verification checkpoint** - Confirms you're approaching the right vehicle
- **Reduces errors** - Prevents hooking up the wrong vehicle
- **Saves time** - Driver can scan parking lot for color first, then verify plate/VIN
- **Communication** - Easy to describe to others ("the silver Honda")

### Impact on Operations:
When a driver pulls into an apartment complex with 50 vehicles, they first scan for the color. "Red Toyota Camry" narrows the search immediately. Color is so distinctive that it's often the difference between a 2-minute location and a 20-minute search. It also serves as an immediate red flag - if the work order says "black" but the vehicle is white, the driver knows to stop and verify before proceeding. Accurate color documentation prevents wrong vehicle recovery and significantly improves operational efficiency.

## **Address** *(Required)*

### Purpose:
The address field provides the complete, accurate street address where the vehicle is currently located. This is the destination the driver will navigate to for vehicle recovery.

### Why This Matters:
The address is the foundation of the entire recovery operation:

- **Driver navigation** - GPS routing depends on accurate address
- **Zone assignment** - Determines which driver/zone handles the recovery
- **Time estimates** - Distance affects scheduling and completion time
- **Route optimization** - Dispatch can group nearby recoveries
- **Legal documentation** - Required for repossession paperwork
- **Proof of location** - Establishes where vehicle was recovered
- **Property jurisdiction** - Determines if private property rules apply
- **Access planning** - Address type indicates potential challenges

An incorrect address means:
- Driver wastes time going to wrong location
- Delayed recovery affects entire schedule
- Increased fuel costs and labor hours
- Customer dissatisfaction
- Potential missed recovery window

### Impact on Operations:
Accurate address information is critical for operational efficiency. A precise address with apartment number, building identifier, or lot section allows drivers to navigate directly to the vehicle. Vague or incorrect addresses can add 30-60 minutes to a recovery as drivers search the area, contact dispatch, or return to the office. In high-volume operations, address accuracy directly impacts how many vehicles can be recovered per shift and affects overall profitability.

## **Reachable** *(Required)*

### Purpose:
The Reachable field indicates whether a tow truck can physically drive up to the vehicle and hook it up immediately, or if there are obstacles (gates, fences, other vehicles, locked garages) that prevent direct access.

### Why This Matters:
This single yes/no question has massive operational implications:

**If Reachable (Yes):**
- Driver can proceed directly to recovery
- Standard time estimate applies
- Normal equipment is sufficient
- Can be scheduled in regular route
- Higher probability of successful recovery

**If Not Reachable (No):**
- May require coordination with property owner
- Might need gate codes or access permission
- Could require special equipment (dollies, flatbed)
- May need to schedule for specific time window
- Might require multiple trips
- Could need additional personnel
- May be delayed or rescheduled

### Impact on Operations:
Accurate reachable status is critical for scheduling and resource allocation. If a vehicle is marked "reachable" but is actually behind a locked gate, the driver makes a wasted trip, loses time, and the vehicle remains unrecovered. If marked "not reachable" when it actually is accessible, the recovery gets lower priority and takes longer to complete than necessary. This field helps dispatch:

- **Prioritize assignments** - Reachable vehicles can be recovered faster
- **Allocate resources** - Not reachable vehicles may need specialized equipment
- **Set expectations** - Clients know if delays are possible
- **Plan routes** - Group reachable vehicles for efficiency
- **Schedule strategically** - Not reachable vehicles might need specific time windows

## **Rusted** *(Required)*

### Purpose:
The Rusted field alerts drivers to significant rust or corrosion on the vehicle that could affect towing safety or cause structural damage during recovery. This is a critical safety and liability indicator.

### Why This Matters:
Rust isn't just cosmetic - it affects structural integrity:

**Safety Concerns:**
- **Tow points may break** - Rusted hooks or frame mounts can snap under towing stress
- **Frame weakness** - Corroded frame rails may not support towing forces
- **Equipment damage** - Broken tow points can damage towing equipment
- **Driver injury** - Sudden equipment failure can cause injury
- **Vehicle damage** - Rusted parts may break off during transport

**Liability Protection:**
- **Pre-existing damage** - Documents rust before towing
- **Prevents false claims** - Owner can't blame company for rust damage
- **Insurance coverage** - Affects claim validity
- **Legal protection** - Proves condition at time of recovery

**Operational Impact:**
- **Different towing method** - May need flatbed instead of wheel-lift
- **Extra inspection time** - Driver must carefully assess before hooking
- **Special equipment** - May need reinforced tow points or straps
- **Slower recovery** - Extra care required

### Impact on Operations:
When a vehicle is marked "rusted," the driver knows to perform a thorough inspection of tow points and frame before attempting recovery. They may need to use alternative hook-up points, bring additional equipment, or call for a flatbed. This advance warning prevents dangerous situations where tow points break during recovery, potentially causing accidents, injuries, or equipment damage. It also protects the company legally - if the vehicle is documented as rusted before towing, the company cannot be held liable for rust-related damage that occurs during recovery.

## **Location Type** *(Required)*

### Purpose:
The Location Type categorizes the property where the vehicle is parked (Single Family Home, Apartment Complex, Commercial Property, Street Parking, etc.). This gives the driver context about the environment they'll be working in and what challenges to expect.

### Why This Matters:
Different location types present different operational challenges:

**Single Family Home:**
- Usually easy access via driveway
- May need homeowner permission
- Potential for dogs or confrontation
- Typically quiet residential area
- Best recovered during daytime

**Apartment Complex:**
- Often gated with access codes needed
- Tight parking spaces
- Multiple residents may interfere
- Parking permits may be required
- Best recovered early morning or late evening

**Commercial Property:**
- May have security guards
- Business hours restrictions
- Employee vehicles blocking access
- Loading zones and delivery schedules
- Best recovered after hours

**Street Parking:**
- Direct access but traffic concerns
- Parking restrictions and permits
- Narrow streets may limit maneuverability
- Public visibility
- Best recovered during low-traffic hours

### Impact on Operations:
Location type helps drivers mentally prepare for the recovery environment. An apartment complex suggests potential for tight spaces, gates, and resident interference - the driver knows to bring gate code information and plan for extra time. A commercial property might require coordination with security. Street parking means watching for traffic and parking enforcement. This advance knowledge allows drivers to:

- **Bring appropriate tools** - Gate code list, dollies for tight spaces
- **Plan timing** - Avoid peak hours at commercial locations
- **Set expectations** - Know if recovery will be quick or complex
- **Prepare mentally** - Anticipate potential challenges
- **Allocate time** - Adjust schedule based on location complexity

## **Parked** *(Required)*

### Purpose:
The Parked field describes how the vehicle is positioned in its parking space (Pulled In, Backed In, Parallel, Angled, Blocked, etc.). This tells the driver which direction the vehicle is facing and how they'll need to approach the hook-up.

### Why This Matters:
Vehicle orientation directly affects towing strategy:

**Pulled In (Front First):**
- Vehicle nose faces wall/building
- Must back vehicle out or use front dollies
- More complex than backed-in position
- Adds 5-10 minutes to recovery
- May need extra space behind vehicle

**Backed In (Rear First):**
- Ideal for towing
- Can hook front wheels and pull straight out
- Fastest recovery method
- Minimal maneuvering needed

**Parallel (Street Parking):**
- Vehicle parallel to curb
- May need to pull forward/back to clear space
- Watch for traffic
- Standard recovery time

**Blocked:**
- Other vehicles prevent direct access
- May need to wait for vehicles to move
- Could require coordination with other owners
- Might need to return later
- Significantly delays recovery

### Impact on Operations:
Knowing the parking position helps drivers visualize the recovery before arriving. "Backed in" means quick and easy - hook and go. "Pulled in" means they'll need to back the vehicle out or bring dollies. "Blocked" means potential delays and might require rescheduling. This information helps with:

- **Time estimation** - Different positions take different amounts of time
- **Equipment planning** - Dollies needed for certain positions
- **Approach strategy** - Driver knows which direction to approach from
- **Mental preparation** - Anticipate complexity level
- **Schedule accuracy** - Adjust time allocations based on parking difficulty

## **Notes: Tires Deflated** *(Checkbox)*

### Purpose:
This checkbox indicates that one or more of the vehicle's tires are flat, significantly under-inflated, or damaged. This is a critical operational flag that changes the entire towing approach.

### Why This Matters:
Deflated tires fundamentally change how a vehicle must be towed:

**Safety Issues:**
- **Cannot tow on flat tires** - Damages rims and is unsafe
- **Rim damage** - Flat tires cause rim-to-pavement contact
- **Uneven weight distribution** - Creates instability during towing
- **Potential blowout** - Partially inflated tires may fail during transport

**Equipment Requirements:**
- **Dollies required** - Must lift deflated wheels off ground
- **Flatbed may be needed** - If multiple tires are flat
- **Extra time** - Dolly setup adds 10-15 minutes
- **Different truck** - Standard wheel-lift may not be sufficient

**Operational Impact:**
- **Changes assignment** - May need driver with dolly equipment
- **Affects scheduling** - Takes longer than standard recovery
- **Increases cost** - Additional equipment and time
- **Requires different approach** - Cannot use standard wheel-lift method

### Impact on Operations:
When "Tires Deflated" is checked, dispatch knows immediately that this is not a standard recovery. They must assign a driver with dollies or a flatbed, allocate extra time, and potentially charge additional fees. Without this information, a driver might arrive with standard equipment, realize they cannot safely tow the vehicle, and have to return to get dollies - wasting an hour or more. This checkbox prevents wasted trips and ensures the right equipment is brought the first time.

## **Notes: Electronic Brake** *(Checkbox)*

### Purpose:
This checkbox indicates the vehicle has an electronic parking brake (EPB) system instead of a traditional manual parking brake. EPBs are common on vehicles manufactured after 2015, especially luxury brands and newer mainstream vehicles.

### Why This Matters:
Electronic parking brakes cannot be released without electrical power:

**Technical Challenge:**
- **Requires power** - Battery must have charge or need jump box
- **May need key** - Some systems require key in ignition
- **Computer controlled** - Cannot manually release like traditional brake
- **No emergency release** - Unlike cable brakes, cannot be manually overridden

**Common on These Vehicles:**
- BMW, Mercedes, Audi (2010+)
- Honda Accord, Civic (2018+)
- Toyota Camry (2018+)
- Tesla (all models)
- Most luxury and electric vehicles

**Driver Must:**
- Bring jump box or portable power supply
- May need to access vehicle interior
- Know specific release procedure for that make/model
- Plan extra 5-10 minutes for brake release

### Impact on Operations:
Without this warning, a driver might arrive, hook up the vehicle, and discover the rear wheels won't roll because the electronic brake is engaged. If they don't have a jump box or can't access the interior, they cannot complete the recovery. This checkbox ensures the driver brings necessary equipment and knows to budget extra time for brake release. It prevents failed recovery attempts and wasted trips.

## **Notes: Mechanical Issue** *(Checkbox)*

### Purpose:
This checkbox indicates visible mechanical problems beyond normal wear and tear - issues like broken suspension, damaged steering components, transmission problems, or other mechanical damage that affects how the vehicle can be towed.

### Why This Matters:
Mechanical issues can make standard towing dangerous or impossible:

**Safety Concerns:**
- **Broken suspension** - Vehicle may not roll properly or could collapse
- **Damaged steering** - Vehicle won't track straight during towing
- **Transmission problems** - May lock wheels or leak fluid
- **Drive shaft issues** - Could fall off during transport
- **Wheel bearing failure** - Wheels may seize or fall off

**Towing Implications:**
- **May require flatbed** - Cannot safely tow with wheels on ground
- **Extra inspection needed** - Driver must assess severity
- **Liability concerns** - Pre-existing damage must be documented
- **Special handling** - May need extra straps or securing methods

**Documentation:**
- **Protects company** - Proves damage existed before towing
- **Prevents claims** - Owner cannot blame company for mechanical damage
- **Insurance purposes** - Affects coverage and claims

### Impact on Operations:
This checkbox alerts the driver to inspect the vehicle carefully before attempting recovery. They may need to take additional photos, use different towing methods, or call for a flatbed. Without this warning, a driver might hook up a vehicle with broken suspension and cause further damage during towing, leading to liability claims. The checkbox ensures proper equipment, extra documentation, and appropriate handling for mechanically compromised vehicles.

## **Notes: Cosmetic Issue** *(Checkbox)*

### Purpose:
This checkbox documents pre-existing body damage, dents, scratches, broken lights, missing parts, or other cosmetic damage visible on the vehicle before towing begins.

### Why This Matters:
Cosmetic damage documentation is critical for liability protection:

**Legal Protection:**
- **Proves pre-existing damage** - Shows damage existed before company touched vehicle
- **Prevents false claims** - Owner cannot claim company caused the damage
- **Insurance defense** - Documentation supports insurance claims
- **Court evidence** - Photos and notes serve as legal proof

**Common Cosmetic Issues:**
- Dents, dings, and scratches
- Broken or missing lights
- Cracked windshield or windows
- Missing bumpers or trim pieces
- Paint damage or fading
- Rust spots or corrosion
- Missing mirrors or door handles

**Driver Responsibility:**
- **Photograph all damage** - Document every dent, scratch, and missing part
- **Note on paperwork** - Written record of condition
- **Compare before/after** - Proves no new damage occurred
- **Detailed documentation** - Specific description of each issue

### Impact on Operations:
This checkbox tells the driver to spend extra time photographing and documenting the vehicle's condition before towing. While this adds 2-5 minutes to the recovery, it provides crucial protection against damage claims. Many vehicle owners claim towing companies damaged their vehicles during recovery. Comprehensive pre-towing documentation proves the damage existed beforehand, protecting the company from false claims and potential lawsuits. This checkbox is essentially an insurance policy against liability.

## **Additional Notes** *(Text Field)*

### Purpose:
The Additional Notes field is a free-form text area where spotters can provide any information that doesn't fit into the structured fields above. This is where detailed observations, special instructions, access information, and unique circumstances are documented.

### Why This Matters:
Every vehicle recovery has unique aspects that cannot be captured in dropdown menus and checkboxes:

**Access Information:**
- Gate codes and entry procedures
- Security guard instructions
- Best time for recovery
- Contact person for access
- Alternative entry points

**Vehicle-Specific Details:**
- Exact location of damage
- Unusual modifications or customizations
- Missing parts inventory
- Interior condition observations
- Odor issues (smoke, mold, pets)

**Location Challenges:**
- Narrow streets or tight spaces
- Steep hills or slopes
- Soft ground or mud conditions
- Traffic patterns and restrictions
- Parking enforcement schedules

**Safety Concerns:**
- Aggressive dogs on property
- Hostile or uncooperative property owner
- Unsafe neighborhood requiring police escort
- Structural hazards (low-hanging wires, unstable ground)
- Weather considerations

**Special Instructions:**
- Client-specific requirements
- Priority level and urgency
- Delivery location specifications
- Key location if available
- Alarm system information

### Impact on Operations:
The Additional Notes field is where spotters share their on-the-ground intelligence with drivers. A note like "Gate code #1234, opens 6 AM-10 PM only" prevents a wasted trip at 11 PM. "Large aggressive dog in yard - owner aware of recovery" prepares the driver for potential confrontation. "Very narrow alley, truck must enter from Oak St" saves the driver from getting stuck. These contextual details transform a basic work order into a comprehensive recovery plan, significantly improving efficiency and success rates.

## **Photos** *(Required - Maximum 5)*

### Purpose:
The Photos field requires spotters to upload 1-5 images that visually document the vehicle's identity, condition, and location. Photos provide verification that cannot be achieved through text alone and serve as legal evidence of vehicle condition.

### Why This Matters:
Photos are the most powerful form of documentation:

**Legal Verification:**
- **VIN photo** - Irrefutable proof of vehicle identity
- **License plate photo** - Visual confirmation of registration
- **Condition documentation** - Proves pre-existing damage
- **Location proof** - Shows where vehicle was recovered
- **Court evidence** - Admissible in legal proceedings

**Operational Benefits:**
- **Driver preparation** - Sees actual vehicle before arriving
- **Verification** - Dispatch can confirm correct vehicle
- **Access assessment** - Shows parking situation and obstacles
- **Equipment planning** - Visual assessment of vehicle size and condition
- **Quality control** - Ensures spotter found correct vehicle

**Risk Management:**
- **Prevents wrong vehicle recovery** - Visual verification catches errors
- **Liability protection** - Documents condition before towing
- **Insurance claims** - Supports damage claims and disputes
- **Customer disputes** - Resolves disagreements about condition
- **Training tool** - Examples for new spotter training

### Required Photo Types:

**1. VIN Plate (MANDATORY):**
- Legal proof of vehicle identity
- Must show all 17 characters clearly
- Taken through windshield from outside
- Most critical photo - recovery cannot proceed without it

**2. Full Vehicle:**
- Overall condition and appearance
- Shows parking position and surroundings
- Includes license plate if possible
- Provides context for recovery

**3. License Plate:**
- Quick visual verification
- Shows plate condition and state
- Confirms registration status
- Helps driver locate vehicle

**4. Damage Documentation:**
- Close-up of any dents, scratches, or damage
- Protects company from false claims
- Shows rust or cosmetic issues
- Documents pre-existing condition

**5. Location/Access:**
- Shows obstacles or access challenges
- Captures gate codes or entry points
- Documents parking situation
- Helps driver plan approach

### Technical Requirements:
- **File formats:** JPG, PNG, or HEIC
- **File size:** Up to 10MB per photo
- **Quantity:** Minimum 1 (VIN), maximum 5 total
- **Quality:** Clear, in focus, well-lit

### Impact on Operations:
Photos are the difference between a smooth recovery and a potential disaster. A clear VIN photo allows dispatch to verify they have the correct vehicle before sending a driver. Damage photos protect the company from thousands of dollars in false damage claims. Location photos help drivers prepare for tight spaces or access challenges. The few minutes spent taking quality photos prevents hours of problems, disputes, and potential legal issues. In the towing industry, photos are not optional - they are essential business protection and operational tools.

## Summary: Why Each Field Matters

Every field in the Spotter Intake Form serves a specific purpose in the vehicle recovery operation:

**Legal Protection Fields:**
- VIN, Photos - Prove correct vehicle and condition
- Cosmetic Issue checkbox - Documents pre-existing damage

**Safety Fields:**
- Rusted, Mechanical Issue - Prevent equipment failure and accidents
- Tires Deflated, Electronic Brake - Ensure proper equipment and procedures

**Operational Efficiency Fields:**
- Address, Location Type, Parked - Enable proper planning and routing
- Reachable - Determines if immediate recovery is possible
- Client - Ensures correct procedures and billing

**Verification Fields:**
- Plate, Year, Make, Model, Color - Confirm correct vehicle identity
- Photos - Visual verification of all information

**Communication Fields:**
- Additional Notes - Shares critical context and instructions
- All checkboxes - Quick flags for common issues

When spotters complete every field accurately and thoroughly, they enable drivers to work efficiently, safely, and successfully. Incomplete or inaccurate information leads to wasted time, failed recoveries, safety issues, and potential legal problems. Each field is a critical piece of the operational puzzle.
